## 1. System Overview

The Pharmacy Management System is a multi-branch business management platform.

The initial implementation will use a Modular Monolith architecture, separating business capabilities into independent modules while keeping deployment and operational complexity manageable.

The architecture must preserve clear module boundaries so individual modules can evolve independently in the future.

## 7. Critical Business Rules

### Inventory

1. Every medicine batch belongs to a medicine and a branch.
2. Every batch has an expiry date and traceable received quantity.
3. Stock must never become negative unless an explicitly approved business exception is defined.
4. Stock deductions must be recorded in the stock movement ledger.
5. FEFO is the default batch allocation strategy.
6. Expired stock must not be sold.
7. Inventory adjustments require a reason and an authorized user.
8. Stock transfers must maintain source and destination traceability.

### Sales

1. Every sale belongs to a branch.
2. Every sale has one or more items.
3. Sale confirmation must be transactional.
4. Inventory deductions and financial records must remain consistent.
5. Completed sales must not be silently modified.
6. Returns must reference the original sale.
7. Sale cancellation and returns must restore stock according to the return inspection process.

### Prescriptions

1. A prescription may belong to a patient.
2. A prescription can contain multiple medicine items.
3. Prescription OCR results require human verification.
4. Dispensing must record the actual dispensed quantity and batch.
5. Partial dispensing must be supported.
6. Prescription records must preserve their history.
7. Prescription-only and restricted medicines require configurable dispensing controls validated against applicable regulations.

### Multi-Branch

1. Every operational transaction belongs to an organization and branch.
2. Users may have access to one or multiple branches.
3. Branch inventory must remain isolated.
4. Cross-branch stock movement must use an explicit transfer workflow.
5. Organization-level reporting must respect user permissions.

## 8. Inventory Ledger Strategy

The system must distinguish between:

- Medicine: The catalog definition.
- Batch: A specific production/expiry batch.
- Inventory Balance: Current available quantity.
- Stock Movement: Historical record of quantity changes.

Stock movements must be auditable and must not be silently deleted or overwritten.

Inventory balances are maintained transactionally and reconciled against the movement ledger.

Concurrency control is required to prevent overselling and double deductions.

## 15. Definition of Done

A feature is complete only when:

1. Its acceptance criteria are satisfied.
2. Business rules are implemented.
3. Input validation is implemented.
4. Authorization is verified.
5. Unit tests are passing.
6. Relevant integration tests are passing.
7. API documentation is updated.
8. Error handling is implemented.
9. Localization is considered.
10. No unresolved critical regression exists.
11. Relevant documentation is updated.

## 16. Delivery Approach

The system will be implemented in independently testable phases.

No module may bypass core security, audit, inventory, or transaction integrity requirements.
